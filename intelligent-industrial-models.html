Frequently Asked Questions

What is a LiDAR Laser Scanner?

A LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) Laser Scanner is a precision instrument that uses laser light to measure distances. It sends out thousands of laser pulses per second, measures how long they take to bounce back, and uses that data to create highly accurate 3D representations of physical environments.

How does it work?

A LiDAR scanner rotates while emitting rapid laser pulses across the environment. When the light pulses hit a surface, they reflect back to the sensor. By calculating the time of flight for each pulse, the scanner determines the exact distance to millions of points, instantly generating a high-density 3D point cloud of the surrounding area.

Where are they used?

LiDAR laser scanners are heavily used in industrial engineering, construction, architecture, and plant management. They are essential for as-built documentation, capturing precise measurements of factories, oil and gas refineries, heritage buildings, and complex infrastructure to enable Scan-to-BIM and reverse engineering workflows.

Accuracy (1mm to 10mm)

Terrestrial LiDAR scanners offer sub-millimeter to millimeter-level precision depending on the specific scanner model and range. For highly detailed engineering and industrial plant scanning, high-end scanners routinely deliver geometric accuracy between 1mm and 4mm, ensuring that the resulting digital twins are highly reliable for clash detection and retrofitting.
