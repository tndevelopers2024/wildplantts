Frequently Asked Questions

What is a LiDAR Laser Scanner?

A LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) Laser Scanner is a precision instrument that uses laser light to measure distances. It sends out thousands of laser pulses per second, measures how long they take to bounce back, and uses that data to create highly accurate 3D representations of physical environments.

How does it work?

LiDAR scanners emit laser pulses towards target objects. The time taken for each pulse to bounce back is measured by the sensor. Combined with internal sensors, the system computes the exact X, Y, and Z coordinates of millions of points per second, forming a detailed 3D point cloud.

Where are they used?

LiDAR is widely used in industrial plant scanning, civil engineering, construction progress tracking (BIM), forestry management, archaeology, autonomous vehicles, and heritage conservation to create high-precision digital twins.

Accuracy (1mm to 10mm)

Our terrestrial 3D scanners deliver survey-grade accuracy ranging from 1mm to 10mm depending on the scanner model, distance, and environmental conditions. This level of precision is ideal for engineering design, clash detection, and quality control.
